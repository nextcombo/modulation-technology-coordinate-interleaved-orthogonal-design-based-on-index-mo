# [Modulation Technology] Coordinate Interleaved Orthogonal Design Based on Index Modulation in the Application of Security Communication [with MATLAB Code]

This paper proposes a physical layer security scheme that applies the novel index modulation (IM) technology to coordinated interleaved orthogonal design (CIOD). The proposed scheme is named CIOD-IM, which leverages the diversity gain of CIOD transmission through index modulation to enhance spectral efficiency. To achieve satisfactory confidentiality rates, this paper designs a special artificial noise matrix that does not affect the performance of legitimate receivers but degrades the performance of eavesdroppers. The paper derives expressions for both the traversable confidentiality rate and the theoretical bit error rate upper limit, considering actual conditions and analyzing the performance under imperfect channel estimation. Computer simulation results show that the proposed scheme outperforms existing IM-based schemes and has the potential to become a candidate solution for future secure communication systems.
In recent years, Indexed Modulation (IM) schemes have attracted widespread attention due to their innovative information transmission methods [1]. Spatial Modulation (SM), as a form of IM, utilizes the index of the transmitting antenna as an additional source of information, marking a significant advancement in traditional Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) transmission technology [1][2]. In spatial modulation systems, by only activating a single antenna during transmission, it avoids Inter-Carrier Interference (ICI) and Inter-Antenna Synchronization (IAS) issues, and requires only a single radio frequency chain, thus reducing hardware costs. However, the broadcast nature of wireless communication channels poses a risk of information being leaked by eavesdroppers. Consequently, physical layer (PHY) security has received attention from researchers in recent years.Physical layer security utilizes the channel characteristics between transmitters and target receivers to prevent information leakage. Both space modulation and physical layer security exploit the randomness and uniqueness of wireless channels, providing new degrees of freedom for solutions based on space modulation.

In this context, this paper proposes a novel physical layer security scheme suitable for Single User (SU) Multi-Input Single-Output (MISO) systems, namely CIOD-IM. The primary design goal of the system is to achieve high spectral efficiency and high diversity gain while ensuring physical layer security through a novel index modulation method and coordinated orthogonal design (CIOD). In this system, a special artificial noise matrix is designed to ensure that artificial noise signals can be cancelled at the target receiver in each time slot. This paper analyzes the bit error rate and ergodic confidentiality rate (ESR) performance under Rayleigh fading channels, considering the challenges in obtaining perfect channel state information in practice. Method Introduction 2.1 Coordinate Interleaved Orthogonal Design (CIOD) This system adopts the coordinate interleaved orthogonal design (CIOD) suitable for MIMO systems, which central idea is to transmit the same phase and orthogonal components of amplitude-phase modulation symbols through different transmit antennas in different time slots to achieve diversity gain. For a specific coordinate interleaved orthogonal design with four transmit antennas, only two radio frequency chains are required per time slot, reducing system complexity. The paper presents the conventional CIOD matrix with dimensions 4x1 and a rate of 1, and points out that full diversity can be achieved only when the Cartesian product distance (CPD) between constellation sets Ω is not zero.To meet this condition, the constellation with zero product distance (such as QAM) needs to be rotated by a certain angle. For square lattice constellations and QPSK, when the rotation angles are 31.7175° and 13.2885° respectively, the product distance reaches its maximum [10]. Another important feature of coordinate interleaved orthogonal design transmission is its symbol-by-symbol detection capability, which reduces the decoding complexity [11].
2.2 The CIOD-IM method considered in this paper is for a large-scale MISO secure communication system, where the transmitter (Alice) has N emitters with M bits each, and both the target receiver (Bob) and eavesdropper (Eve) have single-receiver antennas as shown in Figure 1. Among them, 4log₂(M) bits determine four M-ary amplitude and phase modulation symbols transmitted in different time slots, and log₂(N) - 1 bits select one antenna combination from N/2 possible antenna combinations, thus the spectral efficiency is l = (4log₂(M) + log₂(N) - 1) / 4 bits per channel use (bpcu).
In practical applications, due to channel estimation errors, there may be situations where the channel state information is imperfect. Therefore, this paper assumes that the transmitter and the target receiver are aware of the imperfect channel state information of the main channel (from the transmitter to the target receiver), while the eavesdropping channel (from the transmitter to the eavesdropper) is only known by the eavesdropper, and gives expressions for both the main channel and the eavesdropping channel [7]. The elements of the main channel and the eavesdropping channel are independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) complex Gaussian variables with mean zero and variance 1.

2.2.2 Artificial Noise Cancellation
To achieve artificial noise cancellation, it is necessary to carefully design the artificial noise matrix Zᵢ for the i-th combination according to index modulation, such that it is cancelled through the main channel. The construction of Zᵢ involves replacing θ(x₁̃,x₂̃) and θ(x₃̃,x₄̃) in Sᵢ with Q(z₁₁,z₂₁) and Q(z₁₂,z₂₂), respectively. The expressions for the Q matrices are provided. When selecting the i-th antenna combination, the interference caused by the artificial noise matrix Zᵢ at the target receiver is derived. When this interference is zero, the artificial noise signal is cancelled at the target receiver, thus the target receiver is not affected by the artificial noise matrix.Due to the imperfect channel state information of the known main signal channel in the transmitter, an appropriate expression for the artificial noise coefficient is provided. It should be noted that even if the eavesdropper knows the channel information between the transmitter and the target receiver, they cannot eliminate the impact of the artificial noise.
2.2.3 The received signal vector expression of the target receiver is derived by receiving signals, and after simplification, a simplified form is obtained. The model of the eavesdropper's received signal is essentially the same as that of the target receiver, except for the impact of artificial noise, which also leads to the derivation of the eavesdropper's received signal expression and the processing of the received signal through linear whitening transformation.
2.2.4 Both the detection of the target receiver and the eavesdropper adopt a two-stage detection method, characterized by its ability to detect symbols one by one and low complexity. Conclusion This paper proposes a new confidentiality scheme named CIOD-IM. The scheme enhances the spectral efficiency of coordinated orthogonal design transmission through novel index modulation technology, and achieves satisfactory confidentiality performance through a specially designed artificial noise matrix. The security performance and bit error rate (BER) performance of this scheme were studied in Rayleigh fading environments, and compared with a benchmark scheme. The traversal rates of both the target receiver and eavesdropper were derived, leading to the traversal confidentiality rate, and the theoretical upper bound of BER was deduced, verifying the correctness of the simulation results. Simulation results show that the CIOD-IM scheme outperforms the benchmark scheme in terms of both confidentiality and BER performance. Finally, under large-scale MISO configurations, the BER performance of the CIOD-IM scheme has been improved, indicating that this scheme holds promise as a candidate solution for future large-scale MIMO systems requiring reliability and confidentiality.
